India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA) came into effect with significant implications for any organisation that collects and processes personal data — including schools. This is not a regulation schools can ignore.

Here's a plain-language guide to what DPDPA means for your school and the steps you should take now.

## What data does your school collect that falls under DPDPA?

Schools collect an unusually broad range of personal data:

- **Student data**: name, date of birth, address, Aadhaar number, academic records, health information
- **Parent data**: names, contact details, financial information (for fee collection), employment details (for scholarship verification)
- **Staff data**: biometric attendance, payroll data, performance records
- **Behavioral data**: attendance patterns, disciplinary records, counselling notes

Under DPDPA, all of this is "personal data" and requires a legal basis for processing. For most of it, the legal basis is **consent** — and schools are now required to document that consent.

## Key obligations under DPDPA

### 1. Consent before collection

Schools must obtain explicit, specific, and informed consent before collecting personal data. A blanket admission form consent clause is not sufficient.

What this means practically:
- Create separate consent forms for different purposes (admission, health records, WhatsApp communications)
- Explain clearly what data is collected and why
- Allow parents to withdraw consent (and define what happens to existing data)

### 2. Data localisation (§16)

Under §16 of DPDPA, significant personal data must be stored in India. This is directly relevant to your school ERP vendor — if your data is stored on servers outside India, you are non-compliant.

**Ask your current vendor explicitly**: Where is your data stored? Can they provide evidence of India-based storage?

### 3. Purpose limitation

Data collected for one purpose cannot be used for another without fresh consent. If you collected a parent's mobile number for fee reminders, you cannot use it for marketing your new wing without separate consent.

### 4. Data retention and deletion

Schools must define how long they retain personal data and must delete it when it's no longer needed. You need a documented data retention schedule — including what happens to student records after graduation.

### 5. Data breach notification

In the event of a data breach, you must notify the Data Protection Board within a prescribed timeframe. You need an incident response plan before an incident happens.

## What to do now

**Step 1: Audit your data collection points.** Map every place your school collects personal data — admission forms, fee receipts, attendance systems, health records, WhatsApp groups.

**Step 2: Review your ERP vendor's data practices.** Where is your data stored? What are their breach notification procedures? Do they offer a Data Processing Agreement?

**Step 3: Update your consent processes.** Revise your admission forms to collect specific, documented consent for each data use.

**Step 4: Implement a data retention schedule.** Define how long you keep records for current students, alumni, and staff who have left.

**Step 5: Train your staff.** DPDPA compliance requires organisational culture change, not just process change. Staff who handle student data need to understand why this matters.
